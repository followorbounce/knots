Coiling and storing

Coil it so it runs free next time, and store it where it will last.

ConstructionMaterialsCareCoilingTermsTechnique

A good coil comes out of the bag, off the hook or out of the locker without a tangle. A bad one costs you time just when you need the rope.

Coils

  1. Laid rope: coil with the lay. For right-hand laid rope, coil clockwise. Give each loop a small twist with your fingers so it lies flat.
  2. Braided rope: figure-eight coil. Lay the loops alternately to one side and the other, like an 8. The twists cancel out, so the rope will not kink.
  3. Butterfly coil. Drape the rope back and forth across your shoulders or hand in long bights. Quick, kink-free, and easy to carry as a pack. Common for climbing rope.
  4. Finishing a coil. Wrap the last part of the rope several times round the middle of the coil, pass a bight through the top, and pull the end through that bight.
  5. Flaking. To have rope ready to run, lay it on the ground or deck in a loose pile, end to end, so it pays out without snagging.

Storage

  • Clean and dry first. Natural fibres rot if stored damp; synthetics grow mildew on dirt.
  • Somewhere cool, dark and airy. Sunlight slowly weakens most fibres.
  • Hang coils or keep them in a bag, off the floor and away from chemicals.
  • Seal or whip the ends so they cannot unravel.
