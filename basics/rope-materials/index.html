What rope is made of

Natural and synthetic fibres: which stretch, which float, which slip.

ConstructionMaterialsCareCoilingTermsTechnique

Natural fibres were the only choice until the middle of the twentieth century. Today most working rope is synthetic: stronger for its size, rot-proof, and available with exactly the stretch a job needs.

Choosing

  • Shock loads (towing, anchoring, climbing falls): a rope that stretches, usually nylon.
  • Holding a position (sheets, halyards, guy lines): low stretch, usually polyester.
  • On or in the water (rescue throw lines, water-ski tow ropes): a rope that floats, usually polypropylene.
  • Craft and decorative knots: cotton or soft braided polyester, which take a shape and hold it.

Strength figures on a label are for new rope pulled straight. Knots, bends round small objects, wear, sunlight and age all reduce the strength you can actually use, which is why working loads are set well below breaking loads.
