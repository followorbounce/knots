Best knots for

Climbing

Climbing knots hold people, so they are chosen for security and for how easy they are to check by eye. Learn them from an instructor; use this page to revise.

  • Hitches

    Cow Hitch

    A bight of rope dropped round a rail with both legs pulled through it.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Stopper knots

    Double Overhand Knot

    An overhand knot with one extra turn.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Stopper knots

    Figure-Eight Knot

    The standard stopper.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Loops

    Figure-Eight Loop

    A Figure-Eight Knot tied in a doubled rope.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Bends

    Flat Overhand Bend

    The two ends held together and tied as one in an overhand knot.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Bends

    Water Knot

    An overhand knot in one end, followed back through in reverse by the other end.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Loops

    Bowline with Stopper

    A bowline with its tail tied in an overhand knot round the leg of the eye beside it, so the bowline cannot shake loose when it is not loaded..

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Loops

    Double Englishman’s Loop

    The Englishman’s Loop tied with double overhands.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Bends

    Double Fisherman’s Knot

    Each rope ties a double overhand round the other.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Hitches

    Italian Hitch

    A hitch on a carabiner or bar that lets rope run under control.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Hitches

    Klemheist Knot

    A friction hitch tied with a loop of cord or tape.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Hitches

    Prusik Knot

    A loop of thinner cord wrapped round a rope.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Loops

    Round Turn Bowline

    A bowline with the small loop taken twice round.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Loops

    Threaded Figure-Eight Loop

    The Figure-Eight Loop tied round something you cannot drop a loop over, such as a harness, a tree or a ring: tie a single figure-eight, pass the end round, and trace it back through the knot..

    ●●○ Intermediate
