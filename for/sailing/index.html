Best knots for

Sailing

Boats run on a handful of knots: stoppers for sheet ends, hitches for fenders and moorings, a loop for almost everything else. These are the ones worth knowing without thinking.

  • Loops

    Bowline

    The classic fixed loop.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Binding knots

    Clove Hitch

    Two turns round a post with the second crossing over the first.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Splices & whippings

    Common Whipping

    Turns of thin twine bound round a rope end to stop it unravelling.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Hitches

    Cow Hitch with Toggle

    A cow hitch through a ring where a wooden toggle takes the place of the ends: the bight is caught round the toggle.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Stopper knots

    Figure-Eight Knot

    The standard stopper.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Hitches

    Half Hitch

    The simplest hitch: the end goes round the standing part and across itself.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Hitches

    Marlinspike Hitch

    A loop laid over the line with a spike or rod pushed through it: over the loop, under the line, over the loop.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Hitches

    Pedigree Cow Hitch

    A cow hitch made secure for a pull on one leg only: the free end is tucked back up between the legs and under the bight, so it cannot slide out..

    ●○○ Easy
  • Hitches

    Round Turn and Two Half Hitches

    A round turn on a ring or post takes the load while you finish with two half hitches round the standing part.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Bends

    Sheet Bend

    The everyday knot for joining two ropes, including ropes of different thickness.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Stopper knots

    Slipped Figure-Eight Knot

    A figure-eight finished with a bight, so a pull on the short end releases it.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Binding knots

    Slipped Square Knot

    A square knot with one end pulled through as a bight.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Binding knots

    Square Knot

    A flat binding knot for tying the two ends of one cord round something: a parcel, a bandage, a reefed sail.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Stopper knots

    Stevedore Knot

    A figure-eight with one more turn round the standing part.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Binding knots

    Thief Knot

    It looks almost exactly like a square knot, but the two short ends come out diagonally opposite each other.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Hitches

    Two Half Hitches

    A turn through a ring or round a post, then two half hitches round the standing part, both the same way.

    ●○○ Easy
  • Splices & whippings

    Back Splice

    Finishes the end of a three-strand rope without whipping: the strands are crowned, then each is tucked back down the rope against the lay, over one strand and under the next..

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Loops

    Bowline with Stopper

    A bowline with its tail tied in an overhand knot round the leg of the eye beside it, so the bowline cannot shake loose when it is not loaded..

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Hitches

    Buntline Hitch

    Two half hitches tied back toward the ring instead of away from it, so the second hitch is trapped inside the first.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Bends

    Carrick Bend

    A join for heavy rope, woven strictly over-under.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Braids & sennits

    Carrick Mat

    The carrick bend’s weave closed into a flat oval and followed round twice.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Stopper knots

    Crown Knot

    The strands at the end of a laid rope are folded over each other in turn, so each one passes over the next and under the one before.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Bends

    Double Sheet Bend

    A sheet bend with a second turn of the thinner rope round the bight.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Splices & whippings

    Flat Seizing

    Binds two parts of rope side by side with twine: tight turns round both, then frapping turns between them to draw the turns tight.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Braids & sennits

    Four-Strand Round Sennit

    Four strands braided into a firm round cord.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Hitches

    Mirrored Rolling Hitch

    The rolling hitch tied as its mirror image, for a pull coming from the opposite side.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Stopper knots

    Multiple Overhand Knot

    An overhand knot with four or more turns.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Hitches

    Rolling Hitch

    A hitch that holds against a pull along a pole or another rope, not just across it.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Loops

    Round Turn Bowline

    A bowline with the small loop taken twice round.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Binding knots

    Sheepshank

    Shortens a rope without cutting it: the rope is folded into a Z and each end of the fold is held by a half hitch made in the standing part.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Binding knots

    Turk’s Head (3 lead, 4 bight)

    A woven band round a pole or rail.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Stopper knots

    Wall Knot

    A crown upside down: each strand passes under the next and comes up through its bight, so the ends point up out of the top.

    ●●○ Intermediate
  • Braids & sennits

    Eight-Strand Square Sennit

    Eight strands braided into a thick cord with a square section, each strand passing over two and under two.

    ●●● Advanced
  • Splices & whippings

    Eye Splice

    A permanent eye in the end of a three-strand rope: the end is bent back and its strands are tucked into the standing part, against the lay.

    ●●● Advanced
  • Stopper knots

    Monkey’s Fist

    A woven ball at the end of a line: three sets of turns at right angles to each other, the last set passing inside the first and round the second.

    ●●● Advanced
  • Splices & whippings

    Short Splice

    Joins two three-strand ropes end to end: the ends are married so their strands interlock, then each rope’s strands are tucked into the other rope against the lay.

    ●●● Advanced
  • Binding knots

    Turk’s Head (4 lead, 5 bight)

    A woven band round a pole or rail.

    ●●● Advanced
  • Binding knots

    Turk’s Head (5 lead, 4 bight)

    A woven band round a pole or rail.

    ●●● Advanced
