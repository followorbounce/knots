Splices & whippings

Common Whipping

Also called Plain whipping

Turns of thin twine bound round a rope end to stop it unravelling. Both ends of the twine are buried under the turns.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Lay a bight of twine along the rope, with the loop pointing to the end of the rope.
  2. 2Wrap the twine round the rope and over the bight, working toward the end. Pull each turn tight.
  3. 3Pass the end through the loop of the bight.
  4. 4Pull the other end of the twine to draw the loop and the end under the turns. Trim both ends.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Finishing any rope end that cannot be heat-sealed, such as natural fibre rope.
  • Marking a point on a rope.

Watch out

  • Use strong twine and pull every turn tight.
  • Make the whipping about as long as the rope is thick.
  • For a rope end under heavy use, a sailmaker’s whipping lasts longer.

Untying

Cut through the turns with a knife.
