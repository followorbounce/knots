Stopper knots

Overhand Knot

Also called Thumb knot

The simplest knot there is, and the first move of many others. It makes a small lump in the rope that stops an end slipping through a hole or out of your grip.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Hold the rope with the standing part on the left. The working end on the right is the part that moves.
  2. 2Bring the working end up and round to make a loop, crossing the standing part.
  3. 3Take the end behind the standing part and up through the loop.
  4. 4Pull both ends to draw the knot up. Work the slack out a little at a time so it sits neat.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • A quick stopper in thread, string and cord.
  • Stopping a cut end from unravelling for a short while.
  • The starting move for the Square Knot, the Double Overhand and many fishing knots.

Watch out

  • It jams hard once loaded and can be very difficult to untie in thin line.
  • Any knot weakens a rope; a tight Overhand weakens it more than most.
  • It is small, so it can pull through a hole that a Figure-Eight would block.

Untying

Bend the knot back and forth to loosen it, then push the loop open with a thumbnail or a spike.

Related knots

  • Figure-Eight Knot Bulkier and much easier to untie.
  • Double Overhand one more tuck for a bigger stopper.
  • Slipped Overhand unties with one pull.
