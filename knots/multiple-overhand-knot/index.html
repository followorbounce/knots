Stopper knots

Multiple Overhand Knot

Also called Barrel knot, Blood knot (stopper)

An overhand knot with four or more turns. It forms a long, heavy barrel that adds weight to the end of a line and makes a striking decorative stopper.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Run the working end along beside the standing part.
  2. 2Turn the end back on itself.
  3. 3Wrap it four times round the standing part, working back along it, keeping the turns snug and in order.
  4. 4Tuck the end back through the inside of all the wraps.
  5. 5Hold the turns and pull both ends a little at a time until they close into a smooth barrel.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Weighting the end of a line that has to be thrown.
  • A decorative stopper on cords, pulls and lanyards.
  • A large stopper where a single knot would pull through.

Watch out

  • The more turns, the more carefully you must work the slack out, or the barrel twists.
  • Hard to untie once tight.

Untying

Push the turns apart one at a time from the end that was tucked, and work the end back out.

Related knots

  • Double Overhand Knot The same idea with two turns.
  • Stevedore Knot A large stopper that unties more easily.
