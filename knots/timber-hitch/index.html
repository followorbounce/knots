Timber Hitch

A noose that grips a log, a pole or a bundle: the end goes round the standing part and is twisted back round its own turn. The harder the pull, the tighter it grips, and it falls apart as soon as the load is off.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Bring the rope to the pole from the left, in front.
  2. 2Take it round behind the pole and back to the front.
  3. 3Pass the end round the standing part.
  4. 4Twist the end three times round its own turn, against the pole.
  5. 5Pull the standing part. The turn closes and traps the twists.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Dragging or lifting a log or a pole.
  • Starting a diagonal lashing.
  • Gathering a bundle of canes or sticks.

Watch out

  • It only holds while the standing part is loaded. Add a half hitch further along to keep a pole straight when towing.
  • Make at least three twists; more on smooth or slippery poles.

Untying

Slack the standing part and the twists unroll.
