Cow Hitch

Also called Lark’s head, Girth hitch

A bight of rope dropped round a rail with both legs pulled through it. It is the standard way to fix a loop of cord or a sling to a ring, rail or harness.

  1. 1One leg hangs below the rail.
  2. 2Going up, it passes under the rail to the front, then up the front, over the top and down the back.
  3. 3Behind the rail it crosses behind both legs, just under the rail: this is the bight that holds them.
  4. 4On the other side it goes up the back, over the top, down the front and under the rail, and comes out under the bight as the second leg.
  5. 5Tied with a bight, it is quicker: put the bight over the rail and pull both legs through it.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Fixing a sling or loop of cord to a harness or a ring.
  • Hanging tags, keys and lanyards.
  • A quick tie to a rail when both ends take the load.

Watch out

  • It holds best when both legs are loaded. With only one leg pulled, it can slide.
  • Girth-hitching two slings together weakens them; follow the maker’s instructions for climbing gear.

Untying

Pull the bight open and lift it back over the rail.

Related knots

  • Clove Hitch Two turns the same way instead of opposite.
