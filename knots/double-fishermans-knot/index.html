Double Fisherman’s Knot

Also called Grapevine bend

Each rope ties a double overhand round the other. Pull the two standing parts and the two knots slide together and lock. It is the usual way to join cord into a closed loop.

  1. 1Lay the two ends alongside each other, pointing in opposite directions.
  2. 2Wrap the yellow end twice round both ropes, working back toward its own standing part.
  3. 3Tuck the end back through the inside of both wraps and pull it snug.
  4. 4Do the same with the blue end round the yellow rope: two wraps toward its own standing part...
  5. 5...and tuck it back through the wraps.
  6. 6Pull the two standing parts so the knots slide together and lock.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Knots used to hold people need training. Learn life-safety knots from a qualified instructor, and have a partner check every one before it is loaded.

Use it for

  • Joining the ends of accessory cord to make a sling or a loop for a Prusik.
  • Joining two ropes that must not come apart.
  • Joining slippery or stiff line where simpler bends work loose.

Watch out

  • Leave tails at least several rope-widths long.
  • Once loaded it is close to impossible to untie. Treat the join as permanent.
  • Check that the two halves nest neatly, with the wraps forming matching X patterns.

Untying

Usually you cannot. If it was never loaded hard, pull the two knots apart and pick each one loose.

Related knots

  • Sheet Bend Easy to untie, but much less secure.
  • Prusik Knot Often tied with a loop made by this knot.
  • Fisherman’s Knot single overhands, for light line.
