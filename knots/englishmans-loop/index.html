Englishman’s Loop

Also called Fisherman’s loop

A fixed loop made of two overhand knots, one in the end round the standing part and one in the standing part round the end. Pulled tight, the two knots slide together and lock.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1The standing part runs in from the right.
  2. 2Near where the loop will be, the standing part ties an overhand knot round the end.
  3. 3The rope goes round to form the eye and comes back as the end, through that first knot.
  4. 4The end ties its own overhand knot round the standing part.
  5. 5Pull the standing part and the eye so the two knots slide together and lock.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • A secure loop in thin or slippery line.
  • A loop that holds where a bowline might shake loose.

Watch out

  • It is hard to untie once loaded.
  • Slide the two knots fully together before loading.

Untying

Pull the two knots apart, then pick each one loose.
