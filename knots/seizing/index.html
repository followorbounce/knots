Flat Seizing

Also called Seizing

Binds two parts of rope side by side with twine: tight turns round both, then frapping turns between them to draw the turns tight. It makes a temporary eye, or holds an end back against its standing part.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Lay the end of the twine in the groove between the two ropes.
  2. 2Wrap tight turns round both ropes, over the end, side by side.
  3. 3Take two frapping turns between the ropes, round all the turns, and pull them hard.
  4. 4Finish the end off and trim it.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • A seized eye in the end of a rope.
  • Holding the tail of a knot against its standing part.
  • Binding two lines together where a knot would be too bulky.

Watch out

  • Keep the turns flat and side by side; crossed turns slip.
  • Pull the frapping turns hard; they are what hold it.

Untying

Cut the twine.
