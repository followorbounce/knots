Stopper knots

Figure-Eight Knot

Also called Figure-of-eight, Flemish knot

The standard stopper. It is bulkier than the Overhand, harder to pull through a hole, and it can usually be undone even after a heavy load. Its shape makes it easy to check at a glance.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Hold the standing part at the top. The working end hangs below and does all the moving.
  2. 2Bring the end up and across the standing part to make a loop.
  3. 3Carry the end on round, behind the standing part.
  4. 4Bring it back down through the first loop.
  5. 5Pull the end and the standing part apart to draw the knot into its figure-eight shape.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • The ends of sheets and halyards on a boat, so they cannot run out through a block.
  • The base for the Figure-Eight Loop and the climber’s threaded tie-in.
  • Any rope end you want to stop, then untie later.

Watch out

  • It can shake loose in stiff rope if it is not drawn up firmly.
  • Leave a tail at least a few rope-widths long.

Untying

Push the top loop away from the standing part to break the knot open, then pull the end out.
