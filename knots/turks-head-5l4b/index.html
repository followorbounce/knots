Turk’s Head (5 lead, 4 bight)

Also called 5L4B Turk’s head

A woven band round a pole or rail. Followed round, the single rope crosses itself in a strict over-under pattern: 5 strands lie side by side all the way round (the leads) and the band has 4 scallops on each edge (the bights).

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Wrap the rope round the pole, starting the pattern: each time it meets another part of itself it goes over, then under, then over.
  2. 2Keep following the pattern round. Rotate the pole as you go, so you always work on the front.
  3. 3Keep the over-under strict. One wrong crossing changes the knot.
  4. 4When the end meets the start, the band is complete. Lay the end alongside the start to follow the pattern round again.
  5. 5Tighten gradually all round and tuck the ends under the band.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Decorative grips on tillers, rails, handles and walking sticks.
  • A neckerchief woggle or bracelet when tied round the fingers.
  • Marking the centre spoke of a ship’s wheel.

Watch out

  • Work the slack out a little at a time, all the way round, or the band ends up lopsided.
  • Most are followed round two or three times for a fuller band; tuck the ends inside.

Untying

Loosen it all the way round and slide it off, or work the ends back out.
