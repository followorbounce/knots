Carrick Bend

Also called Sailor’s knot

A join for heavy rope, woven strictly over-under. Laid flat it is one of the most handsome knots there is; under load it capsizes into a compact lump that still unties easily.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Make a loop in the yellow end, with the end crossing over the standing part.
  2. 2Bring the blue end in under the yellow loop and start weaving.
  3. 3Keep going strictly over, under, over, under, crossing its own standing part on the way.
  4. 4Finish with the blue end leaving diagonally opposite the yellow end.
  5. 5Pull the standing parts. Laid flat it stays open; under load it rolls into a tight knot.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Joining heavy ropes, cables and hawsers.
  • A decorative flat knot on its own, or the start of a mat.

Watch out

  • Every crossing must alternate over and under, or it is not a carrick and may slip.
  • The ends can work loose when it capsizes; seize them to the standing parts for a long-term join.

Untying

Push the loops apart; even after a load it opens easily.
