Splices & whippings

Back Splice

Also called End splice

Finishes the end of a three-strand rope without whipping: the strands are crowned, then each is tucked back down the rope against the lay, over one strand and under the next.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Unlay the end of the rope for about a hand-width and tape the strand ends.
  2. 2Crown the three strands so the ends point back down the rope.
  3. 3Tuck each strand in turn against the lay: over the strand next to it, under the one after.
  4. 4Make a second round of tucks the same way, pulling each strand snug.
  5. 5Make a third round. Roll the splice under your foot to even it out, and trim the ends.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • A permanent, chafe-proof end on a three-strand rope.
  • Rope ends that will be handled a lot, such as mooring lines.

Watch out

  • The end becomes thicker and will not pass through a tight block or fairlead.
  • Make at least three full rounds of tucks; four in slippery synthetic rope.
  • Tape or melt the strand ends before you start so they do not fray.

Untying

It is meant to be permanent. Cut it off if you need the end free.
