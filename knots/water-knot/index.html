Water Knot

Also called Ring bend, Tape knot

An overhand knot in one end, followed back through in reverse by the other end. It is the standard way to join flat tape (webbing) into a sling, and works in rope too.

  1. 1Tie a loose overhand knot near the end of the yellow tape.
  2. 2Feed the blue end in where the yellow end comes out, pointing the opposite way.
  3. 3Follow the yellow path backwards, keeping the blue alongside it.
  4. 4Finish tracing the knot. Both ends now lie beside the other’s standing part.
  5. 5Pull all four parts tight. Leave tails a few fingers long.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Knots used to hold people need training. Learn life-safety knots from a qualified instructor, and have a partner check every one before it is loaded.

Use it for

  • Joining tape or webbing into a loop.
  • Joining two ropes or straps that must lie flat.

Watch out

  • Tails can creep back through the knot when the sling is loaded and unloaded. Leave long tails and check them often.
  • Keep the two parts flat and parallel, with no twists.

Untying

Flex the knot hard to loosen it, then pull the ends back out the way they went in.
