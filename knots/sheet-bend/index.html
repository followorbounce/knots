Sheet Bend

Also called Weaver’s knot, Becket bend

The everyday knot for joining two ropes, including ropes of different thickness. The thicker or stiffer rope makes the bight; the thinner one wraps round it.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Make a bight in the thicker rope (yellow).
  2. 2Bring the thinner rope (blue) up through the bight from behind.
  3. 3Take it round behind both legs of the bight.
  4. 4Bring it across the front and tuck it under itself, not into the bight.
  5. 5Hold the bight and pull the blue standing part to set the knot.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Joining two lines to make one longer one.
  • Tying a line to an eye or loop that is already there.
  • Joining rope of different size; the thicker rope always forms the bight.

Watch out

  • It can loosen when it is not under load, especially in stiff or slippery rope.
  • Both short ends should finish on the same side. If they are on opposite sides the knot is weaker.
  • For a big difference in thickness, use the Double Sheet Bend.

Untying

Push the bight of the thicker rope toward the knot to loosen the wrap, then pull the thin rope out.

Related knots

  • Bowline The same structure, tied in one rope to make a loop.
  • Double Fisherman’s Knot Much more secure, but very hard to untie.
  • Double Sheet Bend One more wrap, for ropes of very different size.
