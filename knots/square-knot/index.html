Binding knots

Square Knot

Also called Reef knot

A flat binding knot for tying the two ends of one cord round something: a parcel, a bandage, a reefed sail. It lies flat and comes undone easily. Here it is shown as two interlocked bights, one in each end, so you can see how it is built.

Use it for

  • Tying a bandage or sling flat against the body.
  • Tying round a bundle, a bag neck or a parcel.
  • Reef points on a sail, which is where the other name comes from.

Watch out

  • Never use it to join two ropes that will take a load. It can spill and come apart. Use a Sheet Bend.
  • If both crossings go the same way you get a Granny Knot, which slips and jams. Each rope’s two parts must lie side by side.
  • It is not secure in ropes of different thickness or stiffness.

Untying

Pull one end back sharply along its own standing part. The knot turns into two loose hitches that slide off.

Related knots

  • Sheet Bend The right knot for joining two ropes.
  • Granny Knot the common mistake. It slips.
  • Surgeon’s Knot an extra turn to stop the first half slipping while you tie.
