Prusik Knot

Also called Prusik hitch

A loop of thinner cord wrapped round a rope. It slides when it is loose and grips when it is loaded, in either direction. Named after Karl Prusik, who described it in 1931.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Hold the loop of cord across the main rope (blue). The two legs of the loop point away from you.
  2. 2Wrap the cord round the main rope, working away from the legs, passing it through the inside of the loop each time.
  3. 3Three wraps sit above and three below. The bight of the loop lies across the legs.
  4. 4Dress the wraps so they lie neat and parallel, then pull the legs to set the knot.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Knots used to hold people need training. Learn life-safety knots from a qualified instructor, and have a partner check every one before it is loaded.

Use it for

  • A backup below the belay device when abseiling.
  • Ascending a fixed rope, and hauling and rescue systems.
  • A movable attachment point on a tent or tarp ridge line.

Watch out

  • The cord must be thinner than the main rope. Too close in size and it will not grip.
  • Wet, icy or very new rope grips less. Add a wrap.
  • Test the grip with body weight held safely before you trust it.

Untying

Unload it, then loosen the bight that crosses the legs and unwrap the turns.

Related knots

  • Double Fisherman’s Knot Joins the cord into the loop.
  • Rolling Hitch Grips in one direction, tied with a single end.
  • Klemheist and Bachmann knots other friction hitches.
