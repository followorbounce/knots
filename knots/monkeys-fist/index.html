Stopper knots

Monkey’s Fist

A woven ball at the end of a line: three sets of turns at right angles to each other, the last set passing inside the first and round the second. It weights a heaving line so it can be thrown a long way.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Wrap the rope round your hand three times: the first set of turns.
  2. 2Take three turns round the middle of the first set, at right angles to it.
  3. 3Take three more turns at right angles to both, passing inside the first set and round the second.
  4. 4Put a ball inside if you want weight, then work the slack out turn by turn until the ball is tight.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Weighting the end of a heaving line.
  • Key rings, pulls and decorations.
  • A stopper too big to pull through anything.

Watch out

  • Never put a hard weight inside one that might be thrown at people.
  • Work out the slack in order, following the rope from the standing part to the end.

Untying

Work the slack back from the end through each set of turns in reverse.
