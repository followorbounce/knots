Fisherman’s Knot

Also called Angler’s knot, Englishman’s bend

Each end ties an overhand knot round the other rope. Pull the standing parts and the two knots slide together and jam. Simple, compact and good in thin line.

Drag to turn the knot. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. 1Lay the two ends alongside each other, pointing in opposite directions.
  2. 2Take the yellow end once round both ropes, back toward its own standing part...
  3. 3...and tuck it through, making an overhand knot round the blue rope.
  4. 4Do the same with the blue end round the yellow rope...
  5. 5...and tuck it through.
  6. 6Pull the two standing parts so the knots slide together and lock.

Arrow keys step through. Tags show whether the moving end passes over or under.

Use it for

  • Joining fishing line, string or thin cord.
  • Joining slippery line where a sheet bend would shake loose.

Watch out

  • In thick or stiff rope it is bulky and hard to untie.
  • For anything that holds people, use the double version.
  • Leave tails several times the rope’s thickness.

Untying

Pull the two knots apart, then loosen each overhand.
